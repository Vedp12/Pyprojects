Louisiana's America 250 celebration features free concert, fireworksNews Sports Features Opinion Real Estate Advertise Obituaries eNewspaper Legals NEWSMake plans for free Louisiana America 250 celebration with Grammy winner Greg HilburnShreveport TimesUpdated June 29, 2026, 2:19 p.m. CTLouisiana is hosting a free Fourth of July event in Baton Rouge to begin the celebration of America's 250th birthday.The event will feature live music from Louisiana artists like Wayne Toups, Amanda Shaw and John Schneider.Attendees can also view a rare Louisiana Purchase treaty at the Old State Capitol for free.The celebration concludes with a fireworks show over the Mississippi River.Louisiana's chairman of the America 250 Commission said he expects a crowd of more than 10,000 people to celebrate the Fourth of July Saturday during the state's free signature event in the capital city of Baton Rouge."We began to conceptualize and plan this celebration last year and we believe it's going a tremendous event for everyone," Chairman Mike Bayham said Monday in an interview with USA Today Network.Louisiana's celebration of the nation's 250th birthday begins at 2 p.m. at Repentance Park between the Old State Capitol and the Raising Cane’s River Center and features an afternoon and evening of live entertainment.The Louisiana America 250 Commission and Lt. Gov. Billy Nungesser organized the concert and fireworks show that features some of the state's most beloved artists, including Grammy winner Wayne Toups.Many of Saturday's performers are uniquely Louisianan, from Toups, the "King of “King of ZydeCajun," to famed fiddler Amanda Shaw to Rockin’ Dopsie Jr. & The Zydeco Twisters and country star John Schneider.The Military Concert Band kicks off the concert with rousing patriotic favorites to set the tone for the event.Bayham said there will also be a reading of the Declaration of Independence."We chose genres that represent Louisiana; performers who are a staple of our state," said Bayham, who is also an elected state representative. "Our music is one of the great contributions we've made to the American culture - a diversity of sound and music born here.""It's going to be a nice time with a lineup of some of our state's favorite artists," Nungesser said in an interview with USA Today Network. "We're expecting a big crowd and a great event."Louisiana's celebration of the America's 250th birthday begins at 2 p.m. at Repentance Park between the Old State Capitol and the Raising Cane’s River Center and features an afternoon and evening of live entertainment.Louisiana's Old State Capitol will also be open Saturday for visitors who want to see a rare Louisiana Purchase treaty on loan from the National Archives in Washington, D.C. Admission to the Old State Capitol is also free.The America 250 on the River concert lineup includes:∎ The Military Concert Band (2:15 p.m.): Performing patriotic favorites and honoring the nation’s military heritage through a stirring musical tribute.∎ Amanda Shaw (3:30 p.m.): Louisiana’s celebrated fiddler, singer and songwriter whose unique blend of Cajun, rock, country and pop music has made her a favorite across the Gulf South and beyond.∎ Rockin’ Dopsie Jr. & The Zydeco Twisters (5 p.m.): Internationally acclaimed ambassadors of Louisiana zydeco music known for their high-energy performances and Grammy-nominated recordings.∎ John Schneider (6:30 p.m.): Actor, singer and Louisiana resident best known for his role as Bo Duke on "The Dukes of Hazzard" and for his successful country music career, which includes multiple No. 1 hits.∎ Wayne Toups (8 p.m.): The Grammy Award-winning musician widely recognized as the “King of ZydeCajun,” whose groundbreaking fusion of Cajun music, zydeco and rock has made him one of Louisiana’s most influential and beloved performers for more than four decades.The event concludes at 9 p.m. with the Visit Baton Rouge & WBRZ Fireworks Extravaganza, lighting up the downtown skyline and Mississippi River waterfront."America’s 250th birthday is a once-in-a-generation celebration, and there’s no better place to celebrate than right here in Louisiana,” Nungesser said. “Our state has played an important role in America’s story, and this concert is an opportunity for us to come together and celebrate the freedoms and opportunities that unite us as Americans. We encourage everyone to join us for an unforgettable day of music, patriotism and community.”Greg Hilburn covers state politics for the USA TODAY Network of Louisiana.
