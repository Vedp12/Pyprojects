Will Louisiana teachers see pay raises after a judge's ruling?News Sports Features Opinion Real Estate Advertise Obituaries eNewspaper Legals NEWSLouisiana teachers set for pay bump after ruling, but there's a catch Greg HilburnShreveport TimesJune 29, 2026, 3:02 p.m. CTA Louisiana judge has approved one-year pay stipends for teachers and school support staff.The stipends will be funded by diverting $168 million from the state's public school funding formula.Lawmakers had previously provided similar stipends for three years but did not renew them due to budget constraints.School superintendents oppose the plan, citing potential budget hardships and cuts within their systems.A Louisiana state judge has cleared the way for teachers and school support staff to receive one-year pay raises for the 2026-2027 school year.Baton Rouge Judge Richard “Chip” Moore on June 29 lifted a temporary restraining order blocking Republican Gov. Jeff Landry's plan to provide the stipends by diverting $168 million in public school funding to pay for them.Landry's executive order required a super majority approval from the House and the Senate, which lawmakers approved in results announced June 24.House members voted 76-8 with one abstention (Democrat Adrian Fisher of Monroe) and 20 representatives not voting.Senators voted 37-1 in favor of the plan with Democrat Katrina Jackson-Andrews of Monroe abstaining. Democrat Royce Duplessis was the lone no vote in the Senate.The action takes effect July 1.“While working toward a permanent solution to raise our teacher pay in Louisiana, the Legislature clearly did not want to see a reduction in teacher pay this year," Republican House Speaker Phillip DeVillier and Republican Senate President Cameron Henry said last week in a joint statement.  "We look forward to the recommendations from the MFP task force we created this session.”Lawmakers have provided $2,000 teachers stipends and $1,000 for support workers for the past three years, but they didn't renew the stipends in 2026-2027 because of budget constraints, which prompted Landry's executive order.Landry and legislators had hoped to provide permanent raises through a proposed amendment to the Louisiana Constitution that would have funded the increases by dissolving three education trust funds.But voters rejected that plan in the May election, leaving teachers, lawmakers and the governor empty-handed.That led to Landry's June 2 order to redirect $168 million from the state's school funding formula known as the Minimum Foundation Program (MFP) to pay for stipends of $2,000 for classroom teachers and $1,000 for most support staff.Landry said his action is designed as a bridge to permanent raises through the school funding formula while a new 15-member task force crafts a plan to reorganize the MFP.But school superintendents across the state oppose Landry's emergency stipend plan because they say the reduction to their budgets create hardship and prompt possible cuts within their systems.Greg Hilburn covers state politics for the USA TODAY Network of Louisiana.
